A little about me

Who I Am

Turning curiosity into useful solutions.

I'm Richard Usengimana, a Mathematical Sciences graduate with a specialization in Data Science. I enjoy working at the point where mathematics, technology, and real-world problems meet.

My journey started with Applied Mathematics, where I learned to think carefully about problems, patterns, and relationships. Over time, that interest naturally led me toward data science, machine learning, and research.

I have worked with health, environmental, financial, and other real-world datasets. What I enjoy most is taking data that initially looks complicated and turning it into something people can understand and use to make better decisions.

My research interests include public health analytics, spatial analysis, machine learning, customer analytics, and the responsible use of AI. I am especially interested in projects where technical work can contribute to practical improvements in people's lives.

Research & Exploration

My master's research explored the spatial distribution of child malnutrition in Rwanda by combining DHS data with environmental and socio-demographic indicators. The project strengthened my skills in research design, data integration, spatial statistics, and communicating evidence.

Data & Technology

I work with Python, R, SQL, machine learning, and visualization tools to explore data and build practical analytical solutions. I am particularly interested in predictive modelling, data quality, and turning raw data into useful insights.

Why It Matters

I believe data science is most valuable when it helps people make better decisions. Whether the context is healthcare, finance, education, agriculture, or public policy, I want my work to go beyond producing numbers and contribute to meaningful outcomes.

What keeps me motivated?

I enjoy learning something difficult, breaking it into smaller pieces, and eventually making it work. I am always looking for opportunities to improve my technical skills, collaborate with others, and work on problems that have a purpose beyond the code itself.
